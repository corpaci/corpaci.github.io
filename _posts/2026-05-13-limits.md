Three results. One move.

The Liar collapses truth. Tarski's theorem formalizes why. Gödel shows that even if you give up on defining truth, you still can't prove everything that's true. Different names, different domains — the same diagonal structure underneath.

The alignment problems look the same from inside. Not metaphorically. Structurally: specifying "aligned" wraps around itself the way a truth predicate does. Verifying alignment hits the same ceiling provability does. Optimizing for a proxy of good dissolves the proxy.

The sequence runs from language to logic to computation to values. Each step is the same wall, one level deeper.
